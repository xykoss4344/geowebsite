**Why is South Africa's population unevenly distributed?**

**Introduction:** 
The fossil record indicates humans have inhabited South Africa since prehistoric times, and during the modern era the region was settled by Khoisan and Bantu peoples. Dutch traders landed at the southern tip of present-day South Africa in 1652 and established a stopover point on the spice route between the Netherlands and the Far East, founding the city of Cape Town.

The current population of South Africa is 58,135,722 as of Sunday, August 11, 2019, based on the latest United Nations estimates. South Africa population is equivalent to 0.75% of the [total world population](https://www.worldometers.info/world-population/). The population density in South Africa is 48 per Km2. This is a result of both human and physical factors which will be explored below.

**Human factors: Apartheid (political/ economic factors)**
Apartheid, (Afrikaans: “apartness”) policy that governed relations between [South Africa](https://www.britannica.com/place/South-Africa)’s white minority and nonwhite majority and sanctioned [racial segregation](https://www.britannica.com/topic/racial-segregation) and political and economic [discrimination](https://www.britannica.com/science/discrimination-psychology) against nonwhites. The implementation of [apartheid](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/apartheid), often called “separate development” since the 1960s, was made possible through the [Population Registration Act](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Population-Registration-Act) of 1950, which classified all South Africans as either [Bantu](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Bantu-peoples) (all black Africans), "coloured" (those of mixed race), or white. A fourth category—Asian (Indian and Pakistani)—was later added.
