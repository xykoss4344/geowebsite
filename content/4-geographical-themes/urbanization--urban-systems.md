Urbanization can be defined as the process whereby populations move **from rural to urban areas**, enabling cities and towns to grow. It also refers to the **increase in the number of people living in urban areas** in comparison to rural areas. It can be measured at the global, regional and national **scales**. Urbanization in many industrializing countries is driven by centripetal population movement. Centripetal population movement involves the migration of people from rural areas to urban. Geographers often explain this migration in terms of push and pull factors. Push factors are negative conditions in rural areas that drive people to migrate away. Pull factors are positive conditions in urban areas that attract people.
