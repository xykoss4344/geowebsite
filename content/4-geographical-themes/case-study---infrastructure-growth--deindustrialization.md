**Urban systems growth** - is the increased need to provide access to clean water, sanitation and waste disposal as urban areas grow. If urban areas are to be successful they also need to expand the transport and telecommunications networks (think HCMC!!). Without these features it is difficult to attract new economic activities. Traffic jams, air pollution, unclean water and inadequate telecommunications reflect a failure by those in charge to match   population growth with infrastructure growth.

**Causes and consequences of deindustrialization in "Motor City" (Detroit, USA)** 
Deindustrialization is the long-term, absolute decline in employment in the manufacturing sectors of the economy. It refers to a lose of jobs rather than a decline in productivity. In many MICs and LICs the economies are rapidly growing as a result of outsourcing and & FDI.  In many traditional industrial towns and cities in HICs, urban deindustrialization has occurred. Sheffield in the UK and Detroit in the US are prime examples.
