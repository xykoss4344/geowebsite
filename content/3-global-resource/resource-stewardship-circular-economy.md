1. The predominant **linear** systems approach of ‘take-make-use-waste’ also leads to the “tragedy of the commons”. [**Watch this video**](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1tllkpeh4rxmtsrg7hy9E-ArCytXcTbHS/view?usp=sharing) to be able to explain this theory. Think about how it could be fixed.

There is now divergent thinking that focuses on more sustainable approaches to resource management - to promote **resource stewardship**, where humans act as ‘caretakers’ of natural resources.

2. The **circular economy** is a systems approach to **resource stewardship**. While watching [**these two videos**](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1QtCxARu5aQzX7rKCB9m0vXLtOMNnceGj?usp=sharing):
a) Define the **circular economy**.
b) In what ways is it different from the **linear economy**?

**3. Applying the circular economy approach in practice**
There are many examples already evident. Watch a sample of [**these videos**](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1MXyoDLCovAKfySC6NdqUb8_yMVCmsHbM?usp=sharing) at different scales and a couple of examples from [**this website**](https://www.ellenmacarthurfoundation.org/case-studies) to record some specific examples. 
Which one(s) impressed you most? What challenges do these projects face?

**4. Is the circular economy "a magical fix" to the way we manage resources?** 
Read [this](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1iKXNwxN5mIT-UPYvG3JrulqYCtAqlmjZ7TGd3qWqKLY/edit?usp=sharing) [**article**](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1iKXNwxN5mIT-UPYvG3JrulqYCtAqlmjZ7TGd3qWqKLY/edit?usp=sharing) to highlight some shortfalls of the **circular economy** approach.
